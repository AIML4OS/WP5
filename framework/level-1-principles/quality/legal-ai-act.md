A flexible, principle-based approach to complying with the AI Act is considered most appropriate for official statistics. Where possible, AI Act requirements should be integrated into existing ESS quality and governance processes (CoP, QAF, internal methodological and quality review) rather than a separate compliance framework.

Key points from WP 5.1:

- Compliance requires a risk assessment of specific tasks, taking into account the role of AI in the use case (autonomous, collaborative or supportive AI).
- It is recommended that tasks are not fully automated and that human intervention is provided for monitoring and review.
- Transparency is mandatory regardless of the GSBPM phase. Variables or records processed through AI solutions should be flagged and disseminated via the quality report.
- A first analysis indicates that most current AI/ML systems in production are not high-risk systems; voluntary application of requirements is encouraged.
- NSIs should establish internal procedures to map AI systems against AI Act requirements and evaluate risk levels, embedded in existing ESS governance structures.
