## Ethical principles for the use of AI

The AI HLEG 2019 report identified four key ethical principles for the use of AI, namely:

- Respect for human autonomy. AI systems must not undermine human self-determination, manipulate users or substitute unjustifiably for human judgement.
- Prevention of harm. AI systems should not cause or exacerbate harm and AI usage must be secure, safe and resilient to misuse.
- Fairness. AI systems must avoid discrimination, bias and/or unjust impacts on individuals or groups. Given that official statistics inform policy and resource allocation, even subtle biases can have significant consequences. Proportionality between means and objectives must also be respected.
- Explicability. AI processes should be transparent and understandable. Decisions informed by AI should be explainable to affected and interested parties.

Translating these principles into operational requirements implies ensuring human oversight, technical robustness, privacy protection, transparency in communication, fairness in design and implementation, societal and environmental responsibility, and clear accountability structures.

## Conclusions on ethics

Generally, the ethical considerations regarding the use of AI are either already covered by legal/quality requirements under which statistical offices work or can be incorporated into the already-existing quality frameworks. These in turn are based on the principles of international (and national) guidelines such as FPOS and CoP and under which statistical offices are meant to operate for all their statistical activity, and which have been found to operate successfully. This would imply that a separate AI ethics framework is not necessary and existing ethical, quality and legal structures provide sufficient safeguards. These frameworks have proven effective in safeguarding the integrity of statistical production up to now and there is no obvious reason why they cannot deal with the use of AI, provided current structures are appropriately interpreted and, where required, refined and adapted.

General statistical ethics centre on impartiality, confidentiality, professional independence, and methodological rigour and these foundational principles remain central safeguards for official statistics and are as relevant for AI use as they are for any statistical tool or process the NSI use. For example, professional independence ensures that methodological decisions remain under expert control while transparency and documentation obligations support reviewability. Data protection law restricts profiling and mandates accountability for automated processing. Oversight of AI applications can also be incorporated into existing methodological and governance bodies. Integrating ethical considerations within the quality dimensions is a pragmatic approach as many AI ethical risks, such as bias, unfair treatment, or insufficient transparency, naturally intersect with quality assurance processes, preventing ethical considerations from being treated as purely theoretical concerns. Other aspects of AI usage, such as AI oversight, could also be integrated into existing methodological or quality committees. In sum, while AI introduces new technical tools and challenges, the foundational ethical principles governing official statistics remain fully applicable. The task is not to replace existing frameworks, but to ensure that they are interpreted and applied in ways that effectively address the evolving landscape of AI-enabled statistical production.
