The AI HLEG (2019) identifies four ethical principles for the use of AI:

- **Respect for human autonomy**: AI must not undermine human self-determination or substitute unjustifiably for human judgement.
- **Prevention of harm**: AI usage must be secure, safe and resilient to misuse.
- **Fairness**: avoid discrimination, bias and unjust impacts; respect proportionality between means and objectives.
- **Explicability**: AI processes should be transparent and decisions explainable to affected and interested parties.

WP 5.1 identifies six key ethical risks: production of false statistics, reinforcement of unwanted bias, creation of "transparent" citizens, transfer of responsibility to an algorithm, disclosure of sensitive information, and lack of environmental sustainability.

**Conclusion of WP 5.1:** apart from environmental sustainability, these risks are already covered by established quality and legal frameworks, or these can be adapted. A separate AI ethics framework is therefore not considered necessary; ethical considerations are integrated within the quality dimensions.
