## Motivation and background

An important goal of the European One-stop-shop for Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning for Official Statistics Project (AIML4OS) is to establish a coherent set of capabilities for implementing AI/ML-based solutions. This also includes the development of European standards for the use of AI/ML in the production of official statistics:

"While AI/ML has been tested in many statistical offices over the past ten years, the need for standardisation has become apparent with the increasing maturity of the applications on the one hand and the frequency of their use on the other. This is not a one-size-fits-all solution for all use cases, but standards are valuable up to a certain level. [...] Clearly, blindly applying AI/ML techniques does not suffice when one wants to produce a high quality output." (Footnote: https://cros.ec.europa.eu/book-page/aiml4os-wp5-standards-methodological-and-implementation-frameworks)

WP 5.1 of the AIML4OS project works on those standards for the use of AI/ML in statistics production with regard to quality, ethics and law. The work package focuses on GSBPM phases 4 to 6. Phase 7 is explicitly not considered (however, the latter is the subject of a corresponding project at UNECE level, see here: https://unece.org/sites/default/files/2025-11/HLG2025%20ProjectProposal2026_AI-Readiness.pdf). Nor is the use of AI/ML in statistical offices that is not specific to statistical production to be examined. Of course, generative AI in particular will play an important role in the everyday work of many employees (and is already doing so to an increasing extent). However, the added value of WP 5.1 lies in focusing on the specific features of statistical production and offering standards for this purpose, according to which we want to work in the European Statistical System. We are convinced that the considerations set out below regarding standards in terms of quality, law and ethics always apply and are also sufficient, regardless of whether it is a matter of the step-by-step use of 'classical' statistical methods, machine learning or 'finished' (generative or non-generative) models (e.g. Large Language Models) in the statistical production process. The fundamental requirements and the need for weighing up are the same in all cases. The assessment does not necessarily apply without restriction if the entire production process (or overarching parts of it, e.g. GSBPM phases 4 to 6) is carried out by, for example, agentic AI: in this case, other measures and metrics may be required.

## Standards

Standards make life easier; they provide a reliable, defined framework that guarantees compliance with certain requirements by definition (of the standard) and thus builds trust in the institutions or their products that apply the standard. The glossary of the European Statistics Code of Practice defines ‘standard’ as follows:

“A normative document, established by consensus and approved by a recognised body that provides for common and repeated use, rules, guidelines or characteristics for activities or their results, aimed at the achievement of the optimum degree of order in a given context.”

The benefits of standards are undisputed, even if, naturally, they must always make compromises in special cases due to their standardising approach. UNESCO states in this regard that standards (for AI):

- support organisations in managing risks and building public trust
- provide voluntary guidance on good practice
- demonstrate that AI systems are safe, ethical, and legally compliant
- enable organisations to innovate more efficiently and effectively by reducing costs and time
- enable organisations to increase their efficiency by minimising errors and increasing productivity
- play a crucial role in supporting organisational compliance.

A distinction must be made between different types of standards (ALSO FROM UNESCO): terminology standards that help to build a common language by setting out agreed-upon terms and definitions; process and management standards that define organisational processes and approaches; measurement standards that set metrics and points of reference; testing and performance standards that set out repeatable methodologies and requirements to test the quality, safety, and performance of products and processes; and interface and networking standards that set out requirements for compatibility and interoperability, ensuring that products and systems can work together. For AI in general, there are various groups within standardisation organisations that define standards, including ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 (AI), the IEEE AI Standards Committee, and CEN-CENELEC JTC 21 (AI); see Oviedo et al (2024) and Kilian R et al (2025). The standardised use of AI/ML in production is of great importance for official statistics. Official statistics form the basis for political, business and private decisions. According to Article 2 of Regulation (EC) No 223/2009 on European statistics, official statistics must adhere to the principles of professional independence, impartiality, objectivity, reliability, statistical confidentiality and cost-effectiveness. Furthermore, the development, production and dissemination of European statistics shall take into account international recommendations and best practice. This document presents a framework derived from the quality requirements of official statistics in Europe and, already including some methodological and implementation aspects, that seeks to define a standard for the use of AI/ML in the production of official statistics in Europe.

## Current state of play in NSIs

WP 4 of the AIML4OS project investigated, inter alia, the existence of quality standards for ML processes in NSIs in Europe (plus Canada for example) in 2025. (Footnote: https://aiml4os.github.io/WP4/chapters/survey1.html) In their results, WP 4 noted that 10 of 26 NSIs reported having explicit ML-specific regulations, guidelines, or frameworks in place. These frameworks essentially comprise Data Protection & Compliance Requirements, Methodological & Quality Frameworks, and Roles, Responsibilities & Governance. A large majority of the NSIs that have not yet installed regulations, guidelines, or frameworks are currently discussing the introduction of such. Interestingly, the development and introduction of such regulations is neither a clear bottom-up process (inspired by the needs from daily activities), nor a clear top-down (management's instructions) process.

## Our contribution

Based on existing literature as well as national and international preliminary work on the topics of quality, ethics and law, we are developing a European standard for the use of AI/ML in the production of official statistics here. The aim is to ensure that this standard is incorporated into the European Statistics Code of Practice (Footnote: https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/quality/european-quality-standards/european-statistics-code-of-practice) and thus becomes a binding framework for the official data producers in the European Statistical System. The AIML4OS project provided a good opportunity and the right environment to coordinate between the participating NSIs at the expert level for this purpose. With this, we want to achieve the aforementioned goals, in particular by ensuring appropriate transparency and self-commitment to maintain and strengthen trust in official statistics.
