## Existing frameworks and the production of official statistics

NSIs operate within extensive legal and quality frameworks. The UN FPOS provide a global ethical foundation for statistics, emphasising professional standards, impartiality, and confidentiality. The European CoP operationalises these principles within the European Statistical System, embedding them in detailed quality standards and monitoring mechanisms. National statistical acts, EU Regulation 223/2009, and the GDPR Regulation further safeguard confidentiality, lawful processing and professional independence. The CoP also embeds ethical principles in its quality assurance standards and the Quality Assurance Framework. These help guide NSIs to collect, analyse, and publish data free from political influence, ensure that methods are scientifically sound, and protect the privacy of individuals and organisations. By adhering to these principles, statistical offices maintain public confidence, support informed decision making, and uphold the integrity of national statistical systems.

With respect to AI specifically, the principal regulatory framework in the EU is the Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), which entered into force in August 2024. The Act adopts a risk-based approach, categorising AI systems from minimal to unacceptable risk. The production of official statistics is not automatically classified as high-risk under the Act and typical statistical uses of AI, such as imputation of missing data or classification of survey responses for aggregated outputs, will generally fall into low-or minimal-risk categories, particularly where individual rights or access to services are not affected. Consequently, NSIs are only subject to limited obligations, although transparency requirements remain and where AI generates or manipulates content, this must be clearly labelled. While not mandatory for low-risk systems adherence to ethical guidelines, such as those set out by the AI HLEG, voluntary codes of conduct and participation in initiatives like the AI Pact are encouraged.

## Existing statistical frameworks

As stated previously, official statistics in the EU operate within a framework of national statistical legislation, EU legislation and guidelines and international guidance such as the UN FPOS and the European CoP. Both share the overarching goal of safeguarding the integrity and quality of official statistics.

Statistical offices in the ESS also operate under EU Regulation 223/2009 on European Statistics (updated in 2024) which defines principles for NSIs as partners in the ESS. These principles include professional independence, confidentiality and impartiality. Article 20 of the Act maintains that data collected for statistical purposes is used only in that context and identifies measures to prevent unauthorised disclosure. Statistical offices also operate under various GDPR and Data Protection Acts at national and EU level. For example, the GDPR Regulation (EU 2016/679) mandates that data collected from households or businesses must be strictly confidential and never used for non-statistical purposes. These acts and regulations are obviously relevant for various uses of AI/ML in NSIs.
