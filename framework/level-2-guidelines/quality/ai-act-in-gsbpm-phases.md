At the higher level, the tasks commonly performed in NSIs are grouped and modelled in the GSBPM. For the purposes of our analysis, the focus is on the following phases: Collect, Process, and Analyse (GSBPM phases 4–6 as shown in Figure 1). This focus should be understood as a deliberate scoping decision, rather than an assertion that AI-related risks are confined to these phases. The design and dissemination phases are also relevant to AI risk management. Furthermore, generative AI may indirectly impact phases 4–6 through upstream design choices, external models, or the preparation of outputs and metadata. These aspects are beyond the scope of this study and could be addressed in future work.

In the data collection phase, AI solutions can be adopted to manage the tasks that can benefit from automated routines or scheduled at regular intervals, such as monitoring the data collection. The application of AI models can contribute to identifying or creating statistical units and/or defining the statistical domain of interest, thereby enriching official statistics, reducing respondent burden and improving timeliness. During field operations, AI solutions, like chatbot may assist the respondents to fill in the questionnaire or collect and manage the faqs related to the survey. The development of innovative AI-assisted data collection tools enables respondents to provide information via apps that assist with data entry (e.g. improvements to Household Budget Survey and Time Use Surveys data collection), thereby improving data quality. As the AI systems is designed to interact with people or create content, transparency requirements apply. In the former case, the AI systems s such as chatbots, must be designed to inform the respondent they are interacting with a machine, to enable them to make an informed decision. If the system use respondent biometric or implicit data to infer opinions and statistical information like sex, age, personal traits, ethnicity, and interests, these should be duly informed of the operation of the system and process the data according to and the system shall process the personal data in accordance with Regulations (EU) 2016/679 and (EU) 2018/1725 and Directive (EU) 2016/680, as applicable. In the Process and Analyse phases, which are the most time-consuming stages of the statistical process, AI solutions can be applied to reduce computation time and enrich the information collected. Regardless of the phase, defining process metadata is essential for monitoring the correct application of the AI algorithms and validating the produced output. Providing a description — even a brief, standardised one — of the input, any pre-processing steps, the applied algorithm and the metrics or other indicators used to evaluate the output is fundamental to tracing the process. This approach ensures transparency and enables explainability. In addition, compiling process metadata makes it easier to assess the other quality dimensions and allows for human oversight, thus reducing the risk of biased results.
